The Course Format

A First-Year Seminar at Hopkins

  • 12 students, two hours weekly — genuine intellectual encounter, not content delivery
  • Faculty as interlocutor, not lecturer — students read before we meet; class time is entirely discussion
  • Interdisciplinary by design — economics, history, public health, philosophy, and literature; no single discipline owns the question
03 / 15
Pedagogical Choices

Light Reading, Rich Discussion

  • Short, curated readings — no textbook; texts chosen to reward careful attention and generate discussion, not to cover a field
  • Handwritten in-class response to open each session — 5–8 minutes, then think-pair-share; discussion builds from there
  • Two major assessed projects — Baltimore Progress Video (group, 30%) and Progress Data Project (individual, 30%)
04 / 15
Major Assessment

The Progress Project

Track 1 — Gather New Data
Go find something the world doesn't have yet. Talk to people. Digitize an undigitized archive. Take measurements nobody has taken. The contribution is the data itself — something that did not exist before you produced it.
Track 2 — Make Something
Create progress on something you care about — in your community, for a cause, or personally. AI use is encouraged. You must convince me of relevance and document a trajectory of progress across the semester. The thing has to actually exist at the end.

Why "AI-proof": you either gathered the data or you didn't. The thing either exists at the end of the semester or it doesn't. Neither track can be faked by a language model.

The Other Half of the Title

(And How We Could Lose It)

  • The parenthetical is the intellectual problem — progress is real, unevenly shared, and reversible
  • Between-country inequality has fallen since 1990; within-country inequality has risen — race, class, and place determine who captures the gains
  • Progress reverses — democratic backsliding, opioid crises, climate risk, pandemic setbacks; not footnotes

The Parenthetical, Made Concrete

Progress, Regress, Cycles

1800s–
1940s
Industrial peak: major Atlantic port, Bethlehem Steel at Sparrows Point, one of the largest steel plants in the world; manufacturing and shipbuilding capital
1950s–
1970s
Deindustrialisation, white flight, suburban disinvestment; federal highway policy that bisected Black neighbourhoods; population decline from ~950,000 to ~790,000
1980s–
1990s
Crack epidemic, homicide rates among the highest in the US; disinvestment in public housing and schools; the story of "The Wire," however stylised
2000s–
present
Inner Harbour renewal, Station North arts district, biotech corridor; alongside persistent poverty, the 2015 uprising, and homicide rates that remain high
The
question
Is this a story of progress interrupted, or a story of whose progress counted? The course asks students to hold both possibilities.

Beyond the Seminar Room

Learning by Going

Walters Art Museum
Material culture and human achievement across the ages — what objects tell us about how people understood beauty, craft, and value in earlier centuries
Baltimore Museum of Industry
Baltimore's industrial heritage — labour, manufacturing, and the human cost and gain of the city's industrial century; the textbook data made tangible
Factory Visit (in coordination)
A modern production site — to close the loop between historical industrialisation and contemporary manufacturing; what progress actually builds and who builds it
Practitioner / Guest Speaker
An innovator, entrepreneur, or policy practitioner — bringing the question of how progress happens today into the seminar, live and answerable

What the Course Doesn't Resolve

The Hardest Things to Teach

  • Students who arrive skeptical — often for well-grounded historical reasons; that scepticism deserves a serious answer, not dismissal
  • "Isn't Baltimore proof that progress fails?" — a good-faith question that deserves a good-faith answer; not a pivot to the global aggregate
  • Holding regress without abandoning the frame — opioids, backsliding, climate are not footnotes; how do you teach them alongside the hockey sticks?
