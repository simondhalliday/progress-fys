# FYS: Progress — Print Packet Acquisition Plan

Purpose: build week-by-week PDF packets for device-free class sessions. This tracker separates readings into: already local, open/legal web source, JHU library/course-reserve needed, and still TBD.

Copyright guardrail: use openly licensed/public-domain PDFs where available; use JHU library links, licensed downloads, or course reserves for copyrighted articles and book chapters; do not use pirated PDFs.

## Recommended Folder Pattern

- `print-packets/week01/`
- `print-packets/week02/`
- `print-packets/week03/`
- `print-packets/week04/`
- Continue by instructional week.

Within each folder:

- `00_cover_sheet.pdf` with session date, title, required readings, page expectations, and discussion prompt.
- PDFs numbered in reading order, e.g. `01_rosling_factfulness_intro_ch1.pdf`.
- Optional: `week##_combined_packet.pdf` for printing.

## Padgett and Florence

The new OUP book, *Organizational Invention in Renaissance Florence*, collects Padgett's major Florence work and is organized around economic, political, and kinship networks from 1265–1500. Oxford Academic lists these especially relevant chapters:

- Ch. 1, "Historical Introduction and Overview" (pp. 1–13): best if you want a compact orienting reading to pair with Ada Palmer.
- Ch. 2, "Recombination and Catalysis in Multiple Networks" (pp. 14–27): best theoretical complement to a progress/innovation course.
- Ch. 6, "Transposition and Refunctionality: The Birth of Partnership Systems in Renaissance Florence" (pp. 195–257): strongest substantive chapter for "organizational invention," but too long unless excerpted.
- Ch. 9, "Robust Action and the Rise of the Medici, 1400–1434" (pp. 354–414): famous and politically vivid, but methodologically heavier.

Recommendation: for first-years, pair Palmer's storytelling with either Ch. 1 + selected pages from Ch. 2, or use the earlier article version of Ch. 6:

- John F. Padgett and Paul D. McLean, "Organizational Invention and Elite Transformation: The Birth of Partnership Systems in Renaissance Florence," *American Journal of Sociology* 111, no. 5 (2006): 1463–1568.

That article is the cleanest conceptual match to the course's progress theme because it explains invention as recombination across social, economic, political, and kinship networks. For a print packet, use the introduction, the theoretical framing, and conclusion rather than the full 100+ pages.

Alternative political pairing:

- John F. Padgett and Christopher K. Ansell, "Robust Action and the Rise of the Medici, 1400–1434," *American Journal of Sociology* 98, no. 6 (1993): 1259–1319.

This works well if the Florence session becomes about ambiguity, power, patronage, and network position rather than business organization.
